Gravitational waves meet a thin spherical shell

Flat space inside, Schwarzschild outside. How much of a wave the shell sends back, how it rings, how it changes the polarization, and why it does not want to stay still.

Juan Pablo Elia

Question

Reflection and transmission $\mathcal R(\omega),\mathcal T(\omega)$, quasinormal modes and polarization ($h_+$, $h_\times$) for odd and even gravitational perturbations of the simplest horizonless compact object.

Obstacle

A domain-wall shell always collapses. Which shell can be static, and is it stable?

Answer in one line

A pressure-supported shell: a δ-barrier for axial waves, black-hole-like when compact, ringing with echoes, acting as a polarizing mirror, and slowly unstable.

~30 s. The simplest black-hole mimicker: a membrane of matter with nothing inside. Units G = c = 1; frequencies in units of 1/M. The talk follows a single line of thought: background, junction, scattering, ringing, stability, back to the background, and finally what the two polarizations see.

Toolbox: perturbing a spherical spacetime the ideas used throughout

  • Split the metric: $g=g^{(0)}+h$, with $h$ small. On a spherical background every perturbation is a sum of spherical-harmonic multipoles $(\ell,m)$; waves need $\ell\ge2$.
  • Two parities. Under inversion $\vec x\to-\vec x$, even (polar) pieces pick up $(-1)^\ell$, odd (axial) pieces $(-1)^{\ell+1}$. On a spherical background they never mix.
  • Master functions. For each $(\ell,m,{\rm parity})$ all metric components combine into one gauge-invariant scalar $\Psi(t,r)$ that obeys a 1D wave equation with an effective potential $V(r)$.
  • Tortoise coordinate $r_*$: light moves as $dr_*=\pm dt$, and the horizon sits at $r_*\to-\infty$.
  • Frequency domain: $\Psi\propto e^{-i\omega t}$; frequencies in units of $1/M$, $G=c=1$.
Scattering

Real $\omega$. Send in $e^{-i\omega r_*}$ from infinity and measure what comes back: $\Psi\to B_{\rm in}e^{-i\omega r_*}+B_{\rm out}e^{+i\omega r_*}$. Reflection amplitude $S=B_{\rm out}/B_{\rm in}$, reflectivity $\mathcal R=|S|^2$.

Quasinormal modes (QNMs)

Complex $\omega$ at which a solution is purely outgoing ($B_{\rm in}=0$): the free ringing of the object. ${\rm Re}\,\omega$ is the frequency, $-{\rm Im}\,\omega$ the damping rate. ${\rm Im}\,\omega>0$ would mean a growing mode, an instability. QNMs are the poles of $S(\omega)$.

Black-hole reference

Schwarzschild, $\ell=2$: $\omega M=0.3737-0.0890i$. Waves are reflected by the potential barrier near the photon sphere ($r=3M$) and absorbed by the horizon.

~1.5 min. Everything later uses these words: multipole, parity, master function, tortoise coordinate, reflection amplitude, QNM. Stress that parity is a symmetry of any spherical object, so the two parities are two independent problems. The black-hole numbers are the benchmark we compare to.

The setup one shell, two spacetimes

Questions:

  • Scattering: reflection $\mathcal R(\omega)$ and transmission $\mathcal T(\omega)$ of a gravitational wave, for odd (axial) and even (polar) parity.
  • Ringing: the quasinormal modes (QNMs), complex frequencies with outgoing waves only.

Ingredients:

  • Birkhoff's theorem (every spherical vacuum region is a piece of Schwarzschild) plus regularity at the centre (interior mass $=0$) force a flat interior.
  • Perturbations obey Regge–Wheeler / Zerilli equations outside and the flat wave equation inside.
  • The shell glues the two with junction conditions. That is the hard part.
~1 min. The picture: the shell is a membrane with energy density and pressure. Outside it looks like a black hole of mass M; inside it is empty flat space. Emphasize the time-dilation factor: the same wave has a higher frequency inside, nu = omega/sqrt(f(R)).

Thin shells in general relativity Israel junction conditions

A shell is a hypersurface $\Sigma$ carrying a surface stress tensor $S_{ab}$. Across it:

$$[h_{ab}]=0,\qquad [K_{ab}]-h_{ab}[K]=-8\pi S_{ab}$$

$h_{ab}$: induced metric; $K_{ab}$: extrinsic curvature (how $\Sigma$ bends in each side); $[X]=X_+-X_-$ (Israel 1966; Poisson 2004).

For a spherical shell $r=R(\tau)$ with $\alpha=\sqrt{1+\dot R^2}$, $\beta=\sqrt{f(R)+\dot R^2}$ (Ipser & Sikivie 1984):

$$\sigma=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{4\pi R},\qquad (\alpha+\beta)\ddot R=-\frac{\alpha M}{R^2}+\frac{2p}{\sigma}\frac{\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)}{R}$$

The first equation is the mass formula $M=\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)\,4\pi\sigma R^2$. The second is the shell's equation of motion (tension $\tau=-p$).

Reading the equation of motion
  • Gravity always pulls inward: $-\alpha M/R^2$.
  • Positive pressure pushes outward; tension (negative pressure) pulls inward.
  • Static shell ($\dot R=\ddot R=0$): needs $$\frac{p}{\sigma}=\kappa=\frac{1-\sqrt{f_R}}{4\sqrt{f_R}}>0 .$$

So a static shell needs positive surface pressure, fixed by $R/M$. Dust ($p=0$) and a domain wall ($p=-\sigma$) cannot be static.

~1 min. No need to derive; just the logic: two junction conditions, then the equation of motion. The punchline is the sign: gravity + tension both pull inward.

Waves on each side: one wave equation per parity

$$\frac{d^2\Psi}{dr_*^2}+\big[\omega^2-V(r)\big]\Psi=0,\qquad r_*=r+2M\ln\!\Big(\frac{r}{2M}-1\Big)$$

Gauge-invariant master functions (Martel & Poisson 2005): $\Psi_{\rm odd}$ is built from the axial metric components, $\Psi_{\rm even}$ from the polar ones.

  • odd (Cunningham–Price–Moncrief), Regge–Wheeler potential: $V_{\rm RW}=f\,[\ell(\ell+1)/r^2-6M/r^3]$
  • even (Zerilli–Moncrief): Zerilli potential $V_{\rm Z}$ (Zerilli 1970). For a black hole both give the same QNMs and reflectivity (isospectrality).
  • inside: $\Psi''+[\nu^2-\ell(\ell+1)/r^2]\Psi=0$, so $\Psi=\hat\jmath_\ell(\nu r)$ (Riccati–Bessel)
The interior clock runs slow

$dT=\sqrt{f_R}\,dt$ on the shell, so the same wave has frequency $\nu=\omega/\sqrt{f_R}$ inside: it is blueshifted.

~1 min. In a global tortoise coordinate the whole problem is a 1D wave equation: the flat centrifugal barrier inside, the Schwarzschild barrier outside, and the shell between them. Dotted: what a black hole would have instead. Everything about the shell is in the junction conditions.

How the shell enters the wave equation linearized Israel conditions

Odd parity: a δ-function barrier
$$\Psi_+=\Psi_-,\quad \Big[\frac{d\Psi}{dx}\Big]=\Delta\,\Psi,\quad \Delta=\frac{\sqrt{f_R}\,(1-\sqrt{f_R})}{R}=4\pi\sigma\sqrt{f_R}$$

The same for wall and fluid, and independent of $\omega$ and of the equation of state. The background term $[K]^{(0)}$ cancels against the pressure, and odd waves do not move the matter.

Even parity: a 2×2 transfer matrix
$$\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_+\\ \Psi_+'\end{pmatrix}=\mathsf T(\omega)\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_-\\ \Psi_-'\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det\mathsf T=f_R^{-1/2}$$

Even waves move the shell. Unknowns: radial displacement $\xi_\pm$, surface-density change $\delta\sigma$ (with $\delta p=v_s^2\delta\sigma$) and tangential flow $U$. They are solved together with the wave from 4 metric-continuity and 4 Israel equations. So $\Psi$ jumps, and $\mathsf T$ depends on $\omega,\ell,R/M,\Gamma$. $\det\mathsf T$ is exactly the flux-conservation condition.

Independent test: take a smooth shell of thickness ε (anisotropic fluid, $V\supset4\pi\rho\,e^{2\Phi}$) and let $\varepsilon\to0$.

It converges linearly to the derived $\Delta$, and to no alternative.

~1.5 min. Method: expand the Israel conditions to first order around the static background. Each side is described in its own Regge–Wheeler gauge, so the shell sits at different perturbed positions seen from inside and outside; a perturbed embedding with displacements xi+ and xi- takes care of this. Symbolic algebra throughout. Odd parity is very simple: a repulsive delta potential whose strength is the surface density times the redshift. Even parity is a genuine 2x2 matching.

How much does the shell reflect?

With a regular interior nothing is absorbed, so everything comes back: $|S|=1$ and only the phase of $S$ carries information. To measure how much the object reflects, we open the interior: whatever crosses the shell is let go, as a horizon would.

$$\mathcal R=\Big|\frac{B_{\rm out}}{B_{\rm in}}\Big|^2,\quad \mathcal T=\frac{1}{|B_{\rm in}|^2},\quad \mathcal R+\mathcal T=1$$
  • $\omega\to0$: $\mathcal R\to1$. Long waves are reflected.
  • Compact shells: the transition sits at the barrier top, as for a black hole.
  • Dilute shells: the transition sits at $\omega R\sim\ell$ (flat centrifugal barrier).
~1 min. Emphasize the definition: with the interior opened, the black hole becomes a limit of the same problem. Energy conservation R + T = 1 holds to 1e-10 and is a real test of the junction matrix.

Two limits transparent at high frequency · black-hole-like when compact

$$\omega\to\infty:\quad \mathcal R\simeq\frac{\Delta^2}{4\omega^2+\Delta^2}\ \to0$$

Only the δ-barrier survives. Even parity follows the same law: the fluid's inertia no longer matters.

$$R\to2M:\quad \Delta\to0,\quad \mathcal R\to\mathcal R_{\rm BH}\ \text{(Vishveshwara 1970)}$$

The shell sinks into the redshifted throat. The approach is slow, $\propto(R-2M)^{1/2}$.

~1 min. Left: log-log, both parities collapse onto the delta-barrier law; the dip in the odd curve is an interference between the barrier and the shell reflections. Right: the reflectivity tends to the black-hole one. Keep this in mind: the reflectivity has a black-hole limit, but the ringing does not.

How the shell rings quasinormal modes

QNMs: the regular interior solution, carried through the shell, is purely outgoing at infinity.

  • Cavity modes, $\omega\sim1/R$. For dilute shells, $\omega R\approx n\pi-\tfrac{i}{2}\ln[2\ell(\ell+1)R/M]$: a leaky box.
  • Trapped modes for $R<3M$: caught between the interior and the photon-sphere barrier. ${\rm Im}\,\omega M\approx-10^{-6}$ at $R=2.01M$. These are the echoes.
  • Matter modes (even): oscillations of the shell itself, $\omega\sim(M/R^3)^{1/2}$.

None of them tends to the black-hole QNMs (stars) as $R\to2M$. The black hole rings because waves leak into the horizon; the shell sends them back.

~1 min. Contrast with the previous slide: the reflectivity is continuous in the black-hole limit, the spectrum is not. Trapped modes become exponentially long-lived as the shell approaches 2M.

The static shell is unstable even-parity matter modes

Four matter modes per $\ell$. One is purely imaginary with ${\rm Im}\,\omega>0$:

$$\omega\,(R^3/M)^{1/2}=0.608\,i\qquad(M/R=0.2,\ \Gamma=2,\ \ell=2)$$
  • It agrees with Pitre, Schneider & Poisson (2026), who used a completely different method, at every compactness.
  • Newtonian origin: a pressure-supported self-gravitating membrane buckles. $\varsigma^4-S\varsigma^2+P=0$ gives $1.505$ and $0.515\,i$ (stars).
  • The growth time is $\simeq1.6\,(R^3/M)^{1/2}$: slow compared with the waves.
~1 min. The shell is held up by tangential pressure against its own gravity; l >= 2 deformations run away. This is ordinary Newtonian physics, and GR shifts the numbers by O(M/R). A by-product: the constant term of PSP26's quartic (7.6) contains a misprint; their closed form (7.8) is right.

fluid shell Instability rate $0.6(M/R^3)^{1/2}\ll$ wave frequencies. Scattering and QNMs are a good adiabatic idealization.

frozen wall Collapses on $R/\sqrt2$, as fast as the waves. Its even junction is inconsistent (8 equations, 6 unknowns). Which 2 equations you drop changes $\mathcal R$ at $O(1)$.

~1 min. This closes the loop. For the frozen wall the odd sector is identical to the fluid shell (the delta barrier), but the even sector is undetermined by the approximation: from total reflection to the delta law depending on the prescription. It would need a genuinely time-dependent treatment.

From $\Psi_{\rm even}$, $\Psi_{\rm odd}$ to $h_+$, $h_\times$ what a detector measures

Far away (Martel & Poisson 2005, Eqs. 6.13–6.16):

$$h_+-ih_\times=\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{\ell m}\sqrt{\tfrac{(\ell+2)!}{(\ell-2)!}}\,\big(\Psi^{\ell m}_{\rm even}-i\,\Psi^{\ell m}_{\rm odd}\big)\;{}_{-2}Y^{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$$
$$\dot E=\frac{1}{64\pi}\sum_{\ell m}\tfrac{(\ell+2)!}{(\ell-2)!}\Big(|\dot\Psi^{\ell m}_{\rm even}|^2+|\dot\Psi^{\ell m}_{\rm odd}|^2\Big)$$
  • Parity is not polarization: each parity produces both $h_+$ and $h_\times$.
  • Duality: the factor $-i$ means the odd field is the even one with the polarization rotated by $45^\circ$. They are $E$- and $B$-type patterns (gradient and curl).
  • Only for $m=0$ is even $=h_+$ and odd $=h_\times$ literally.
  • Both parities carry energy with the same weight.
~1 min. ${}_{-2}Y$ are the spin-weighted spherical harmonics, the natural basis for a spin-2 field on the sky. For the l = 2, m = 2 mode of a binary, the even mode gives the familiar (1 + cos^2 theta) h+ and 2 cos theta hx; the odd mode has them swapped. At the equator even is pure plus and odd is pure cross.

Can reflection turn $h_\times$ into $h_+$? a selection rule and a birefringent mirror

Selection rule

The object is spherical and parity invariant, so even and odd never mix. An axisymmetric pure-$h_\times$ (odd) wave always comes back as pure $h_\times$, for a shell, a star or a black hole.

For one $(\ell,m)$, reflection acts on the $(E,B)$ components as a Jones matrix:

$$\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{\rm even}\\ \Psi_{\rm odd}\end{pmatrix}_{\rm out}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{\rm even}&0\\0&S_{\rm odd}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{\rm even}\\ \Psi_{\rm odd}\end{pmatrix}_{\rm in},\qquad \Phi=\arg\frac{S_{\rm even}}{S_{\rm odd}}$$

A wave with equal $E$ and $B$ content (e.g. $h_+=h_\times$ at $m=0$) comes back with a fraction $\sin^2(\Phi/2)$ of its power in the orthogonal polarization. The same fraction flips the helicity.

$$\text{black hole:}\quad \frac{S_{\rm even}}{S_{\rm odd}}=\frac{\lambda+12iM\omega}{\lambda-12iM\omega},\qquad \lambda=(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)$$
~1.5 min. Parity is conserved, polarization is not. The object acts on the two parities like a birefringent crystal: same or different amplitude, different phase. For the black hole the ratio follows exactly from the Chandrasekhar transformation, which maps Regge–Wheeler solutions into Zerilli solutions. Isospectrality means equal moduli, not equal phases: about 21 degrees of retardance at the l = 2 QNM frequency. Numerically verified to 5e-8.

How much polarization changes shells vs black hole

Low frequency: universal. Long waves only see the exterior field, so every object has the black-hole retardance. At the even-only matter resonance conversion is complete; elsewhere a few per cent (up to 19% at $R=2.2M$).

$$\sigma_{\rm flip}=\frac{\pi}{\omega^2}\sum_{\ell}(2\ell+1)\frac{|S_{\rm even}-S_{\rm odd}|^2}{4}$$ $$\xrightarrow{\ \omega\to0\ }\ 144\pi M^2\sum_{\ell\ge2}\frac{2\ell+1}{\lambda_\ell^2}=\frac{4\pi}{3}M^2$$

Helicity-reversing cross section of a circularly polarized plane wave: the same for any spherical object at long wavelengths, and equal to the classic Schwarzschild result.

~1 min. The sum over multipoles is exactly 1/108, which gives 4 pi M^2 / 3, the helicity-reversing part of the long-wavelength cross section M^2 [cos^8 + sin^8](theta/2) / sin^4(theta/2). Numerically: 4.188 M^2 for all objects at omega M = 0.02.

The same incident $h$ pulse, carried by $\Psi_{\rm even}$ or by $\Psi_{\rm odd}$

For one $(\ell,m)$, $h_+(t)$ and $h_\times(t)$ in any direction are a fixed angular factor times $\Psi(u)$. So these curves are the reflected $h$. The prompt reflections almost coincide; the trapped modes differ ($0.421-0.035i$ odd, $0.447-0.044i$ even) and dephase.

Only the even channel keeps ringing at the matter frequency (decay time $\sim3000M$ for $R=3M$) and excites the unstable mode, added causally from the pole of $S_{\rm even}$. It is weakly excited but takes over after $\sim74$–$440M$.

~1 min. Waveforms are built by Fourier synthesis of S(omega) times the pulse spectrum, on adaptive frequency grids that resolve the narrow resonances. The odd channel agrees with an independent time-domain evolution to better than 1%. The growing mode requires moving the Fourier contour above its pole at omega = i gamma.

Is one parity more important?

Energy: neither

Equal weights in the flux; a plane wave contains both equally; a non-absorbing object returns all the energy in both ($|S_{\rm even}|=|S_{\rm odd}|=1$).

Physics: even is richer

Only even waves move the shell and compress its matter: matter resonances, the instability, and the dependence on the equation of state. Odd waves see a universal δ-barrier.

Sources: even dominates

For slow sources even $\ell=2$ is the mass quadrupole. The odd current quadrupole carries $\frac{1}{36}\big(\frac{m_1-m_2}{m}\big)^2v^2$ of the power for a binary; radial infall excites only even modes.

~1 min. With an absorbing interior the two parities also differ in strength: near the barrier top the even one is reflected up to about 18 times more (R = 6M). Practical rule: keep both for scattering of external waves; for slow sources even dominates; the odd sector is the simple and universal one, a good first test of any code.

Take-home messages

  1. A thin shell with flat interior is static only with positive pressure $p=\sigma(1-\sqrt{f_R})/4\sqrt{f_R}$. Domain walls and dust collapse.
  2. For odd waves the shell is a repulsive δ-potential, $\Delta=\sqrt{f_R}(1-\sqrt{f_R})/R$. For even waves it is a flux-conserving 2×2 matrix that carries the shell's own dynamics.
  3. $\mathcal R(\omega)$: opaque at low frequency, transparent at high ($\propto\omega^{-2}$), black-hole-like as $R\to2M$.
  4. The ringing is not black-hole-like: cavity modes, long-lived trapped modes (echoes), and matter modes.
  5. The static shell is unstable (a Newtonian buckling mode) but slowly, so the results stand. The frozen domain wall is not a controlled model.
  6. Parity is conserved, polarization is not: any spherical object is a birefringent mirror. Universal at long wavelengths ($\sigma_{\rm flip}=4\pi M^2/3$), complete at the shell's matter resonance.
  7. Neither parity dominates the energy; the even one carries all the matter physics and dominates slow sources.
Methods

Linearized Israel junction conditions derived symbolically; transfer-matrix and shooting solvers (continued fraction, DOP853); Leaver and WKB benchmarks; time-domain leapfrog evolution; Fourier synthesis with causal treatment of the unstable mode.
