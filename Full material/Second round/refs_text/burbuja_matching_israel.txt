Gravitational-Wave Scattering off a Thin-Shell Vacuum Bubble

            Part I: Israel Junction Conditions and the Background Dynamics

Goal. A thin spherical shell (domain wall) separates flat interior (r < R) from Schwarzschild exterior
(r > R, mass M ). We want the reflection and transmission coefficients for a gravitational wave incident
from infinity, scattering off the shell, with the interior solution regular at r = 0 (constructed previously)
and the exterior carrying both an incoming and an outgoing mode. This requires (i) the linearized Israel
junction conditions relating the jump in the perturbed extrinsic curvature to the perturbed surface
stress-energy of the shell, and (ii) a background (unperturbed) configuration to linearize around.

    Status of this document. Part I below is complete and self-contained: the general thin-shell for-
malism, the vacuum-bubble (domain-wall) stress tensor, and -- critically -- the background dynamics
of a spherical domain wall, which turns out not to admit a static configuration. This changes the shape
of the scattering problem and is why Part II (the actual linearized matching and the R, T formulas)
is deferred to a follow-up once we agree on how to handle this. See the discussion in Sec. 5 and the
question at the end.

4 Consequence for the scattering problem, and a proposed way for-
     ward

A stationary (single-frequency, time-independent-background) scattering calculation of the kind we
set up for the interior wave equation presupposes a static boundary at r = R. Section 3.1 shows
this is impossible for an idealized pure-tension domain wall with nonzero M : the shell is intrinsically
dynamical (it collapses). Three ways to proceed, in increasing order of departure from "the vacuum
bubble, exactly as in Ipser & Sikivie":

1. Adiabatic / high-frequency approximation. Freeze the background at the turning point

R = Rm, R = 0 [Eq. (10)], and compute R(), T () as if the shell were momentarily at

rest there -- valid when the gravitational wave's period is much shorter than the collapse

timescale,   |R¨/R | near Rm (formally infinite right at the turning point, so more precisely

 × (wave-crossing time of the shell region)  1, the usual eikonal/short-wavelength condition).

This  keeps  R  =  0  but  not  R¨  =  0,  so  the  background  K     jump  retains  an  R¨-dependent  piece

                                                                    

that must be kept in the linearized matching (it feeds into the trace term hab[] of Eq. (3) at the

perturbed level).

2. Relax the pure-tension condition. Allow  =  (not a strict domain wall, but a general
   isotropic shell) and look for a genuine static solution of Eq. (8) (R = R¨ = 0 for all time, i.e.

   a true equilibrium, not just a turning point). This exists for special combinations of  /, M ,

   R (Ipser & Sikivie discuss the analogous planar case in their Sec. IV B, finding static solutions
   require  =  or  = 14  for the planar wall [1]; the spherical analogue would need to be worked
   out). This sacrifices the "pure vacuum bubble" character of the shell.

3. Fully dynamical scattering. Keep R(t) evolving self-consistently (collapsing) and treat the

   wave scattering off a genuinely moving/accelerating boundary -- a much harder, intrinsically time-

   dependent problem (no fixed ), likely requiring numerical evolution or a slow-motion (multiple-

    Option 1 is the natural "next step" that stays closest to the literal vacuum-bubble setup and still
yields clean reflection/transmission coefficients (functions of , l) rather than a fully time-dependent
scattering problem. We recommend this and will use it unless told otherwise.

5.1 Why the domain wall adds no new degrees of freedom (odd parity)

Because Sab = -hab with  exactly constant (Sec. 2), the perturbation of the surface stress tensor is
entirely determined by the perturbation of the induced metric:

                                                     Sab = - hab.                                 (11)

There is no independent "surface fluid" variable to solve for (no surface velocity perturbation, no density

perturbation) -- unlike, e.g., a shell of ordinary matter with its own equation of state. This is what

makes the odd-parity sector tractable as a genuinely closed linear problem.

interior note: the odd-parity embedding function vanishes identically), so  remains exactly at r = R.
