Final project · gravitational waves

Gravitational waves meet a thin spherical shell

Flat space inside, Schwarzschild outside. How much of a wave is reflected, how the object rings, and why the shell does not want to stay still.

What was computed

Junction conditions for odd and even perturbations, reflection and transmission coefficients $\mathcal R(\omega),\mathcal T(\omega)$, and quasinormal modes, for two shell models.

How it was checked

Symbolic derivations, two numerical methods, a second independent code, a thick-shell limit, a Newtonian limit, and comparison with Pitre, Schneider & Poisson (2026).

~30 s. Introduce the problem: the simplest horizonless compact object. First round = the work being presented; second round = independent verification. Units G = c = 1, frequencies in units of 1/M.

The setup one shell, two spacetimes

Questions:

  • Scattering: reflection $\mathcal R(\omega)$ and transmission $\mathcal T(\omega)$ of a gravitational wave, for odd (axial) and even (polar) parity.
  • Ringing: the quasinormal modes (QNMs), complex frequencies with outgoing waves only.

Ingredients:

  • Birkhoff's theorem plus regularity at the centre force a flat interior.
  • Perturbations obey Regge–Wheeler / Zerilli equations outside and the flat wave equation inside.
  • The shell glues the two with junction conditions. That is the hard part.
~1 min. The picture: the shell is a membrane with energy density and pressure. Outside it looks like a black hole of mass M; inside it is empty flat space. Emphasize the time-dilation factor: the same wave has a higher frequency inside, nu = omega/sqrt(f(R)).

Thin shells in general relativity Israel junction conditions

A shell is a hypersurface $\Sigma$ carrying a surface stress tensor $S_{ab}$. Across it:

$$[h_{ab}]=0,\qquad [K_{ab}]-h_{ab}[K]=-8\pi S_{ab}$$

$h_{ab}$: induced metric; $K_{ab}$: extrinsic curvature (how $\Sigma$ bends in each side); $[X]=X_+-X_-$ (Israel 1966; Poisson 2004).

For a spherical shell $r=R(\tau)$ with $\alpha=\sqrt{1+\dot R^2}$, $\beta=\sqrt{f(R)+\dot R^2}$ (Ipser & Sikivie 1984):

$$\sigma=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{4\pi R},\qquad (\alpha+\beta)\ddot R=-\frac{\alpha M}{R^2}+\frac{2p}{\sigma}\frac{\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)}{R}$$

The first equation is the mass formula $M=\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)\,4\pi\sigma R^2$. The second is the shell's equation of motion (tension $\tau=-p$).

Reading the equation of motion
  • Gravity always pulls inward: $-\alpha M/R^2$.
  • Positive pressure pushes outward; tension (negative pressure) pulls inward.
  • Static shell ($\dot R=\ddot R=0$): needs $$\frac{p}{\sigma}=\kappa=\frac{1-\sqrt{f_R}}{4\sqrt{f_R}}>0 .$$

So a static shell needs positive surface pressure, fixed by $R/M$. Dust ($p=0$) and a domain wall ($p=-\sigma$) cannot be static.

~1 min. No need to derive; just the logic: two junction conditions, then the equation of motion. The punchline is the sign: gravity + tension both pull inward.

Perturbations: one wave equation per parity

$$\frac{d^2\Psi}{dr_*^2}+\big[\omega^2-V(r)\big]\Psi=0,\qquad r_*=r+2M\ln\!\Big(\frac{r}{2M}-1\Big)$$

Martel & Poisson (2005) gauge-invariant master functions.

  • odd (Cunningham–Price–Moncrief): $V_{\rm RW}=f\big[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}-\frac{6M}{r^3}\big]$ (Regge–Wheeler 1957)
  • even (Zerilli–Moncrief): $V_{\rm Z}$ (Zerilli 1970)
  • inside: $\Psi''+\big[\nu^2-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}\big]\Psi=0$, regular solution $\Psi=\hat\jmath_\ell(\nu r)$ (Riccati–Bessel).

All metric–master-function relations were re-derived by brute-force linearisation of the Einstein tensor and checked symbolically.

~1 min. In the global tortoise coordinate the problem is a 1D Schrödinger-like equation: the Schwarzschild barrier outside, the flat centrifugal barrier inside, and a kink or jump at the shell. The shell's effect is entirely in the junction conditions.

The shell in the wave equation linearized Israel conditions

Odd parity: a δ-function barrier
$$\Psi_+=\Psi_-,\qquad \Big[\frac{d\Psi}{dx}\Big]=\Delta_{\rm odd}\,\Psi,\qquad \Delta_{\rm odd}=\frac{\sqrt{f_R}\,(1-\sqrt{f_R})}{R}$$

Independent of $\omega$ and of the equation of state; the same for models A and C. The worrying background term $[K]^{(0)}$ cancels against the pressure.

Even parity: a 2×2 transfer matrix
$$\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_+\\ \Psi_+'\end{pmatrix}=\mathsf T(\omega)\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_-\\ \Psi_-'\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det\mathsf T=f_R^{-1/2}$$

The shell moves radially by different amounts in the two gauges and its matter oscillates: $\Psi$ jumps, $\mathsf T$ depends on $\omega$, $\ell$, $R/M$, $\Gamma$, and flux is conserved exactly.

Second round: a smooth shell of thickness ε converges to the δ-barrier junction, linearly in ε, and not to wrong alternatives. This is an independent derivation of the odd junction.

~1 min. Odd parity is beautifully simple: the shell is a repulsive delta potential. Even parity is a genuine 2x2 matching; it cannot be written as a single jump. The determinant identity is what guarantees R + T = 1; it is not imposed, so it is a strong test.

Reflection and transmission

The closed system reflects everything ($|S|=1$). To define $\mathcal R,\mathcal T$ we open the interior: waves that enter never come back, like a horizon.

$$\mathcal R=\Big|\frac{B_{\rm out}}{B_{\rm in}}\Big|^2,\quad \mathcal T=\frac{1}{|B_{\rm in}|^2},\quad \mathcal R+\mathcal T=1$$
  • low frequency: $\mathcal R\to1$ (opaque)
  • high frequency: $\mathcal R\simeq\Delta_{\rm odd}^2/4\omega^2$ (a transparent δ-barrier)
  • $R\to2M$: $\mathcal R\to\mathcal R_{\rm BH}$ (Vishveshwara 1970), slowly
  • blue: odd; orange: even, model C (same as odd at high $\omega$); gray: black hole; green: model A (see slide 14)
~1 min. Blue odd, orange even model C, gray black hole. The transition happens at the barrier top for compact shells and at omega R ~ l for dilute ones. Ignore the green curves (model A) for now; we come back to them in the verification part.

Quasinormal modes wave, trapped and matter modes

QNM condition: the Wronskian of the interior-through-shell solution and the outgoing one vanishes.

  • Wave modes ($\omega\sim1/R$). For dilute shells, $\omega R\approx n\pi-i\xi$ with $\xi=\tfrac12\ln[2\ell(\ell+1)R/M]$ (PSP26): a leaky cavity.
  • Trapped modes for $R<3M$: long-lived, behind the barrier; they produce echoes.
  • The Schwarzschild QNMs (dotted) are not a limit of the shell spectrum.
  • Matter modes (even, model C): four per $\ell$, $\omega\sim(M/R^3)^{1/2}$.
~1 min. Wave modes live in the interior cavity. Compact shells trap modes below the barrier; their damping is tunnelling-suppressed, and they are what produces echoes. Matter modes are oscillations of the shell itself.

The static shell is unstable matter modes, model C

One even-parity matter mode is purely imaginary with ${\rm Im}\,\omega>0$:

$$\omega\,(R^3/M)^{1/2}=0.608\,i$$

($M/R=0.2$, $\Gamma=2$, $\ell=2$). The deformation grows exponentially: the shell is dynamically unstable.

  • This matches Pitre, Schneider & Poisson (2026) at all compactnesses and all $\Gamma$ (orange vs blue crosses).
  • Newtonian limit $0.5147\,i$ and $1.5050$ (stars), derived from scratch in the second round.
  • The growth time $\sim1.6(R^3/M)^{1/2}$ is much longer than the wave periods, so scattering off model C is a good adiabatic idealization.
~1 min. Orange: first round (full GR). Blue crosses: read off PSP26. Star: Newtonian limit. The instability is Newtonian in origin: the tangential pressure that holds the shell up makes l >= 2 deformations run away. A byproduct of the check: a misprint in PSP26 Eq. (7.6).

Why the frozen domain wall (model A) is not predictive

The frozen wall is not a solution, so the 8 even-parity junction equations for 6 unknowns are inconsistent. We must pick which 2 of the 4 Israel equations to drop.

  • Different, equally defensible choices give reflectivities from total reflection down to the δ-barrier law.
  • With the first round's choice the energy error exceeds $\mathcal R$ itself.
  • The lowest QNMs shift by 20–30%.

Conclusion: the even-parity model-A results are not determined by the approximation. The odd sector, and all of model C, are unaffected.

~45 s. This is the one substantive physics correction. The wall collapses too fast (omega_dyn ~ 1/R) and the truncation of an inconsistent system is ambiguous at O(1).

Take-home messages

  1. A thin shell cannot be static unless it has positive pressure $p=\kappa(R)\sigma$. Domain walls and dust collapse.
  2. For odd waves the shell is a repulsive δ-potential, $\Delta_{\rm odd}=\sqrt{f_R}(1-\sqrt{f_R})/R$. For even waves it is a flux-conserving 2×2 transfer matrix.
  3. $\mathcal R(\omega)$: opaque at low frequency, transparent at high frequency, black-hole-like as $R\to2M$.
  4. QNMs: cavity wave modes, trapped (echo) modes, and matter modes. One matter mode grows, so the static shell is unstable.
  5. Everything was reproduced independently. The frozen domain wall is the exception: it is not a controlled approximation.
